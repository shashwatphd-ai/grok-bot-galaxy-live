# Day 1 Chronicle — September 15, 2026

The full story of Day 1 at Grok Bot Galaxy, in order. Times are Pacific. Drawn from the complete broadcast recording (8h45m) and the full audio transcript.

## 8:30 AM — Opening: three builders, no idea

Lauren, Roshan and Matt Palmer opened from the studio next to Dreamforce at Moscone Center with the day's premise: build an entire company in three days, with AI agents — Grok Bot — as the workforce. They said it plainly: they did not yet know what company they would build. Before anything else came the announcement of a viewer challenge ending September 29 — share how you've integrated Grok Bot, with a template link, and win a trip to a Starship launch at Starbase, Texas; runners-up get a tour of the SpaceX rocket factory in Hawthorne.

## 9:00–10:00 AM — Grok Bot 101

Roman and Rita walked through the product from zero. Creating your first bot happens through onboarding categories — data & analysis, research & figures, workflows & routines, or "something else, I'll help you name and setup." A Marketplace surfaced community-built bots with creator bylines and install buttons ("Lenny's APIs by Lenny", "Diana's Tips by Diana"), previewing the app-store direction the product is heading. An email demo showed a bot handling a supplier negotiation: a 500-unit shipment confirmation for a winter promotion, drafted and sent.

## 10:15–10:50 AM — The idea: a restaurant pop-up

The idea-selection dialogue was the morning's real drama. The humans rejected the agents' first directions, and the agents came back with candidates: a food company, a high-ticket sales play, an event-book concept, a ticket-craft concept. Out of that exchange the trio locked their choice: **run a real restaurant pop-up in San Francisco** — find the location, partner with a chef, ticket the experience — and build the operating software live: event pages, table-time bookings, payments, sign-ups. Working name: "Pop-up OS."

## 10:55–11:45 AM — Business plan, and Cody Sanchez on distribution

The agents produced a full business plan — product, pricing tiers, marketing strategy, objectives — with a scoping checklist across marketing, sales, operations, finance, legal and engineering. Then guest Cody Sanchez (business-acquisition creator, ~15M followers) joined and delivered the day's most quotable stretch: sell the thing to three people before you build it; distribution is the moat ("the number one reason AI startups don't make it"); controversy marketing works — her AI-generated "move to Austin" video pulled 2.4M views, and a "no paid ads until $5–10M" take pulled 5.7M views and 2,000 signups. Her proof-vault habit — saving every good thing anyone ever says about your product — resonated enough that the trio created a marketing bot live on air, seeded with her advice.

## 12:30–2:00 PM — Grok Bot for Engineering

Lingxi Li's session put the integration stack on screen: GitHub, Notion, Figma and Slack, plus first-party Cursor Cloud Agents management. The demo showed a multi-agent software loop — a finished PR, a deploy handled by an agent with config and logs, review queues — and then a live debugging moment: a stuck cloud agent, diagnosed as "using a subagent… still chasing the PR, not the spinner." A product spec line surfaced in the thread: users should be able to create their own event, with date/time, sessions, venue and staff.

Meanwhile the trio's own build advanced: a domain purchased, a landing page heading to staging, tater installed as the engineering bot, and a rule set that drew a laugh from the chat — "no pull requests, we ship to main" — immediately tested by a 2,000-line PR.

## 2:30–3:30 PM — Grok Bot for Product Managers

Kevin Niparko's session carried the product thesis — "not one omniscient bot, but a full roster of teammates" — on three pillars: referencability (you know who does what), scoped memory (agents learn their jobs), and parallelism (many agents working at once). The demo showed the management experience: a request in plain English, an analyst agent answering with data-linked RFCs, a chief-of-staff agent keeping cadence, a designer agent picking up the handoff.

## 4:00–5:30 PM — Grok Bot for Founders

Shub Gaur's session zoomed out and then made it concrete. The **AI Maturity Curve** charted the industry: ask (chatbots) → do a task (copilots) → automate a job (a bot) → staff a function (a team of bots). The **Why Grok Bot** grid showed the product surfaces: iMessage-simple bot chat, always-on 24/7, computer use with your own logins, shareable templates ("Peng shared Kenny with you"). The **Founder Use Cases**: close customers on autopilot, QA and course-correct, adapt to competitors faster than they respond, ship feedback quickly. And the honesty moment: **"Optimizing Costs — browser use is powerful (and expensive!)"**, an agent unit-economics walkthrough that aired while the broadcast crossed one million views.

## The build, all day

Between and during the sessions, the pop-up company accumulated real artifacts: a booking dashboard, pricing RFCs with live metrics attached, a price menu with web-sourced imagery, night-market ticket stubs and badge/lanyard designs drawn in CSS, Grok Bot plush concepts iterated against official art, a Tee Bot running promo emails, and an SEO work order with a dated execution plan running through Thursday. The bot fleet grew from the original eleven named characters to thirty-plus, with ten archived — the org churned staff all day. Total build time from wireframe to a staged sign-up page: roughly forty-five minutes.

## 6:00 PM — Close

Day 1 ended at 1.1M cumulative views. What exists at the close: a chosen and planned business, a staging site with a sign-up flow, designed tickets and merchandise, a marketing channel, and a dated growth plan — with no public launch and no customers yet. Day 2 opens at 8:30 AM with the sales track: Sales Engineering at 9:00, Sales at 12:30, SDRs at 2:30, Customer Support at 4:00.

**A note on the numbers quoted on stream:** the dollar figures that appeared — per-flight prices, a weekend's revenue, a $30k profit target — came from the build's own staging systems and stated ambitions. No public product launched on Day 1 and no customers have paid; treat the day's economics as a demo economy with internally consistent numbers.
